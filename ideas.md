# Proofroom Standalone Design Direction

## Chosen Direction: The Proofroom

**Design Movement:** Swiss editorial modernism interpreted as an interview rehearsal dossier.

**Core Principles:** Keep one question in focus; make evidence visible; separate live practice from debrief; treat completion color as earned rather than decorative.

**Color Philosophy:** Warm archival paper creates calm concentration. Blue-black ink carries authority, cobalt marks analysis, Room Red identifies live-round moments, and Proof Lime appears only for verified evidence and completed work.

**Layout Paradigm:** An asymmetric studio transcript with a dominant editorial reading column, indexed lesson rail, dark prompt panels, and margin annotations rather than a centered card grid.

**Signature Elements:** Question apertures, transcript rules, room-status signals, and deterministic field plates for proof stories, mock-interview flow, and scorecard comparison.

**Interaction Philosophy:** Notes save locally, lesson changes stay quick, and completion states respond visibly without decorative delay.

**Animation:** Use short opacity and translation entrances only for major page regions. Keep course controls nearly instant and respect reduced-motion preferences.

**Typography System:** Newsreader for editorial display, Manrope for interface and reading copy, and IBM Plex Mono for annotations, source markers, and status labels.

**Brand Essence:** A rigorous rehearsal studio for job seekers who want Copilot to listen before it coaches. Personality: candid, composed, exacting.

**Brand Voice:** Headlines challenge weak habits; calls to action name the next real behavior; microcopy stays specific and calm. “A question list is not a rehearsal.” “Practice the room. Keep the evidence.”

**Wordmark & Logo:** Proofroom uses an angular open-door symbol paired with an editorial wordmark treatment.

**Signature Brand Color:** Room Red, used only for live-practice signals and instruction drift that needs attention.
