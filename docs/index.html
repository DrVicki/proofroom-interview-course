The practice problem

A question list is not a rehearsal.

Generic prompts often ask, answer, and coach in the same breath. That lets you recognize a polished response without practicing the harder work: retrieving evidence, explaining judgment, and surviving a real follow-up.

“A useful mock interview listens before it coaches.”
The rehearsal protocol

One question. One answer. One evidence-aware follow-up.

  1. 01
    Brief

    Map the interview target

    Separate explicit requirements, likely themes, proof areas, and unsupported assumptions.

    Room context
  2. 02
    Stories

    Organize verified proof

    Preserve ownership, decisions, constraints, results, and the facts a follow-up can test.

    Answer evidence
  3. 03
    Debrief

    Keep one fixed rubric

    Compare relevance, specificity, ownership, evidence, judgment, reflection, and clarity.

    Training loop

The evidence standard

Coach the response—not a fictional candidate.

  • ✓

    Listen before coaching. Keep model answers and scoring outside the live round.

  • ✓

    Probe what was said. Follow up on evidence, ownership, judgment, or reflection.

  • ✓

    Leave gaps visible. Use a verified fact request instead of inventing a stronger story.
